1. The South African Municipal Crisis Speaker 1: Problem Framing

South Africa's 257 municipalities face systemic basic service breakdowns. Communities suffer from frequent dry taps, raw sewage overflows, and electrical blackouts.

The Operational Bottlenecks

  • Aging Asset Infrastructure: Reticulation piping networks routinely exceed 35–45 years in service life.
  • Overloaded Electrical Substations: Dense settlements frequently operate substations at 120% to 150% rated capacity.
  • The Reactive Failure Cycle: Municipalities dispatch repair teams only after complete asset collapse or violent public protest.
  • Inequitable Backlogs: Peripheral wards suffer extended downtime compared to central metro cores.

The Data Science Opportunity

  • 30-Day Forward Prediction Horizon: Forecast risk tiers weeks in advance of infrastructure failure.
  • Proactive Maintenance Triage: Pre-deploy water tankers, mobile generators, and engineering work crews.
  • Economic Efficiency: Preventative maintenance costs up to 70% less than emergency structural excavation.
  • Equitable Resource Allocation: Objective algorithmic prioritization removes political bias from service dispatch.
Speaker 1 Note: "Rather than waiting for dry taps or street protests, our system equips municipal infrastructure directors with forward-looking risk intelligence to intervene 30 days before failure."

2. Decision Framing & Cost Asymmetry Speaker 1: Cost Matrix Analysis

We formulate an actionable 3-tier classification problem designed for municipal engineering directors and SALGA:

Tier Classification Share Recommended Municipal Action Plan
Tier 0 Low Risk / Routine Service 70.2% Maintain standard scheduled preventive maintenance cycles. No emergency allocation.
Tier 1 Moderate Vulnerability 21.6% Dispatch technical inspection team within 14–30 days. Assess pipe pressure and substation oil.
Tier 2 Critical Outage Risk 8.1% Immediate 24–48h emergency intervention. Pre-position water tankers and auxiliary transformers.

Decision-Theoretic Cost Matrix ($Cost(FN) \gg Cost(FP)$)

False Positive (Type I Error): Inspecting a stable ward incurs ~R5,000 in technician travel and diagnostic time.

False Negative (Type II Error): Missing an impending failure results in flooded roads, cholera contamination, school closures, and civil protests costing upwards of R500,000.

Metric Selection: We optimize for Macro-F1 and Critical Class Recall rather than raw overall accuracy.

3. POPIA Compliance & Ethical Governance Speaker 1 -> Speaker 2 Hand-Off

POPIA Compliance (Act No. 4 of 2013)

  • Zero Citizen PII: No citizen names, identity numbers, phone numbers, or residential addresses are ever collected or processed.
  • Spatial Aggregation: All demographic and telemetry data is aggregated strictly to municipal ward centroids (>1,000 households).
  • Data Integrity: Datasets are cryptographically signed, versioned, and stored without personal leakage.

Mitigating the "Logging-Bias" Trap

  • The Problem: Affluent suburban wards log hundreds of digital tickets via mobile apps, whereas informal settlements have limited smartphone penetration.
  • Our Mitigation: We discarded raw ticket logs and grounded the model in physical asset telemetry: substation loading factor, pipe age, and census access rates.
  • Human-in-the-Loop: The model functions strictly as an advisory decision-support instrument.

7. Leakage-Free Pipeline & Split-Before-Fit Speaker 3: M Mammburu (12:00 - 18:00)

To eliminate data leakage, we enforced a strict split-before-fit design:

Strict 80/20 Stratified Partitioning

Dataset partitioned into 2,800 Training Wards and 700 Held-Out Test Wards prior to computing any statistical moments, medians, or encoders. Stratification guarantees identical class ratios across splits.

Numeric Transformation Sub-Pipeline

  • Imputation: SimpleImputer(strategy='median') fitted exclusively on train folds.
  • Domain Feature Engineering: Custom transformer computing interaction ratios.
  • Scaling: RobustScaler() using interquartile range to resist extreme outlier distortion.

Categorical Transformation Sub-Pipeline

  • Imputation: SimpleImputer(strategy='most_frequent').
  • Encoding: OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown='ignore') preventing crashes on novel municipalities.
  • Composed into unified Scikit-Learn ColumnTransformer.

8. Domain Feature Engineering Speaker 3: Engineered Features

We engineered three domain interaction features to provide explicit physical context to the learning algorithms:

1. Infrastructure Strain Index

ISI = (Load × Age) / (1 + log(1 + Budget))

Multiplies electrical substation load by asset age, scaled inversely by municipal maintenance budget. Highlights aged, overloaded assets starved of capital.

2. Socio-Economic Vulnerability

SEVR = (Informal% × Unemp%) / (1 + Water%)

Captures compound social risk where high informal dwelling density and unemployment intersect with lack of in-home piped water.

3. Service Adequacy Index

SAI = 0.35(W) + 0.35(E) + 0.15(S) + 0.15(R)

A balanced index measuring municipal compliance across the four basic constitutional services (Water, Electricity, Sanitation, Refuse).

Why XGBoost Outperformed Deep Neural Networks

Tabular data lacks spatial or sequential smoothness. XGBoost's recursive axis-aligned decision trees split tabular continuous boundaries cleanly, whereas neural networks struggle with heterogeneous feature scales and discrete tabular interactions without enormous parameter counts.

15. Paired McNemar Statistical Significance Test Speaker 5 -> Speaker 6 Hand-Off

We tested whether the Champion (XGBoost) differs significantly from the strongest comparator (Logistic Regression) on the 700 test wards:

Contingency Table (700 Wards) Logistic Regression Correct Logistic Regression Incorrect
XGBoost Correct 573 (81.9%) [Both Correct] 45 (6.4%) [XGBoost Only]
XGBoost Incorrect 44 (6.3%) [Logistic Regression Only] 38 (5.4%) [Both Incorrect]

Hypothesis & Exact Test Decision

  • Null Hypothesis ($H_0$): Both models possess equal marginal error proportions ($P(b) = P(c)$).
  • Alternative Hypothesis ($H_1$): Error proportions are significantly different ($lpha = 0.05$).
  • Edwards Continuity-Corrected $\chi^2$: 0.0000 | Exact p-value: 1.0000
  • Statistical Decision: Fail to reject $H_0$ at $lpha = 0.05$. Discordant errors are balanced (45 vs 44).
  • Practical Value: While marginal misclassification rates are balanced, XGBoost provides superior non-linear calibration and minority recall on Critical Risk.
